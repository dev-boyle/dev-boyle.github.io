About the Ensemble

Three cellos and piano create a surprisingly orchestral sound.

Instead of functioning as three identical instruments, the players continually exchange roles:

  • one carries the melody

  • one supplies counterpoint

  • one supports the harmony

  • the piano acts almost like a miniature orchestra

What to Listen For

Try to identify each player's musical personality.

Even on identical instruments, experienced performers develop distinct voices through:

  • tone color

  • vibrato

  • phrasing

  • articulation

  • bow speed

By the second half of the concert, you may find yourself recognizing individual players simply by their sound.

Sergei Rachmaninoff — Vocalise (1915)

Background

Originally written for voice and piano, Vocalise contains no words.

The singer performs entirely on a single vowel sound, making the melody itself the emotional message.

The cello has become one of the work's most natural homes because its expressive range closely resembles the human voice.

Listen For

  • Long, uninterrupted melodic lines

  • Rich late-Romantic harmonies

  • Quiet emotional intensity

  • The melody flowing naturally between the three cellos

  • The piano creating an almost shimmering orchestral backdrop

If performed well, you'll almost forget the piece was ever written for a singer.

Beethoven — Variations on "Là ci darem la mano" (1795)

Background

The theme comes from Mozart's Don Giovanni.

Don Giovanni attempts to charm Zerlina by singing:

"Là ci darem la mano"

"There we'll join hands."

Beethoven originally wrote these variations for two oboes and English horn, making them an excellent fit for three cellos.

This performance uses an arrangement by Blaise Déjardin, principal cellist of the Boston Symphony Orchestra.

Listen For

  • the original Mozart melody

  • playful exchanges between instruments

  • imitation

  • brilliant technical passages

  • Classical elegance

  • increasingly inventive variations

Note

Beethoven never loses sight of Mozart's tune. Every variation transforms it while keeping it recognizable.

Spoken Word

Placed in the center of the concert, the spoken word performance functions as an emotional pivot.

It allows the audience to shift from listening to music toward listening to language before returning to music again.

Depending on the text, it may provide a natural bridge into the reflective world of Popper's Requiem.

Elizabeth Raum — Ballade and Dance

About the Composer

Elizabeth Raum (born 1945) is one of Canada's most frequently performed living composers.

Before composing full time she served as principal oboist of the Regina Symphony Orchestra.

Her music is known for being:

  • lyrical

  • tonal

  • rhythmically engaging

  • emotionally direct

  • audience-friendly

Rather than embracing highly experimental modernism, Raum writes music that communicates immediately.

Ballade

Expect:

  • broad singing melodies

  • warmth

  • reflection

  • expressive lyricism

Dance

Expect:

  • rhythmic vitality

  • playful interaction

  • folk-inspired energy

  • lively exchanges among the three cellos

Musical Relatives

Raum's style occasionally recalls:

  • Aaron Copland — spacious harmonies

  • Samuel Barber — lyrical warmth

  • Malcolm Arnold — rhythmic energy

without directly imitating any of them.

David Popper — Requiem, Op. 66 (1892)

About the Composer

David Popper (1843–1913) was one of the greatest virtuoso cellists of the nineteenth century.

Although modern audiences mostly know him through his famous cello études, he was also a gifted Romantic composer.

He wrote Requiem in memory of his friend and publisher Daniel Rahter.

Unlike many of his virtuosic works, Requiem is deeply personal.

Listen For

  • solemn opening

  • rich cello harmonies

  • organ-like sonorities

  • long lyrical melodies

  • moments where the three cellos seem to breathe together

The piano supports rather than dominates, allowing the cellos to carry the emotional weight.

Important

This is likely to be the emotional centerpiece of the concert.

Themes Running Through the Evening

Melody

Every work values beautiful melodic writing.

Conversation

Especially in Beethoven, listen for musical dialogue rather than soloist and accompaniment.

Blend

Three cellos often sound less like individual instruments and more like a choir or small string orchestra.

Contrast

The evening alternates between intimacy and brilliance, reflection and energy.

The Piano

The piano functions as:

  • harmonic foundation

  • rhythmic engine

  • orchestral color

  • dramatic support

rather than simply accompaniment.

Final Reflection

This is not simply a concert showcasing three excellent cellists.

It is an exploration of what makes the cello one of the most expressive instruments ever created.

Across more than two centuries of music, the program demonstrates the instrument's remarkable ability to sing, converse, dance, comfort, and mourn.

By the final notes of Popper's Requiem, you've heard the cello speak in nearly every emotional language it possesses.
